# Witsand Context & Writing Guidelines

This document contains the established lore, recurring characters, and style guidelines for The Witsand Observer. Use this as a reference when writing new articles.

---

## Geography & Setting

### The Basics
- **Witsand** ("White Sand"): A small coastal village in the Western Cape, South Africa
- **Population**: Approximately 600 permanent residents (swells during holidays)
- **Location**: At the mouth of the Breede River, where it meets the Indian Ocean
- **Nearest Municipality**: Hessequa Municipality, based in Riversdale (~70km away)
- **Known For**: Whale watching, fishing, white sand beaches, and being delightfully remote

### Key Locations
- **The Harbour**: Centre of fishing activity and local gossip
- **The Beach**: Dramatic white sand dunes; the village's namesake
- **The Breede River Mouth**: Excellent fishing; subject of ongoing debates about who caught what
- **The Main Road**: There's basically one
- **The Shop**: You know the one

### The 70km Problem
- All official business requires driving 70km to Riversdale
- This distance is a source of endless frustration and dark humour
- Any municipal decision takes approximately three times longer to affect Witsand
- "We'll get someone out there" is a phrase residents have learned to distrust

## Recurring Characters

These are archetypes that can appear in stories. Give them names and quotes as needed.

### The Old-Timer
- Has lived in Witsand for 40+ years
- Remembers "when this was all just beach" (it still mostly is)
- Sceptical of change, newcomers, and anything from "the city"
- Excellent source of dubious historical facts

### The Newcomer
- Moved from Cape Town/Johannesburg seeking "the quiet life"
- Still learning the unwritten rules
- Either loved or tolerated depending on how they handle the adjustment
- Confused by local customs

### The WSWF Spokesperson
- Representative of the Witsand Sovereign Wealth Fund
- Speaks in vague but confident terms about "individual sovereignty" and "sound money principles"
- Always has "no comment" on how much Bitcoin anyone holds
- Surprisingly available for interviews about anything else

### The Man at the Garage
- Knows everyone and everything
- Information broker of the village
- Speaks in short, knowing sentences
- Neither confirms nor denies anything directly

### The Fisherman
- Professional opinions on: the weather, the fish, the municipality, tourists
- Source of quotes on any topic
- Speaks in metaphors about the sea that somehow apply to everything

### Oom Piet
- The reliable source
- Everyone knows Oom Piet, though he is never directly quoted
- "Piet says..." is sufficient attribution for any rumour

## Running Jokes & Themes

### Ongoing Tensions
1. **Municipality Distance**: 70km is too far for everything
2. **Newcomers vs Old-Timers**: Different expectations of village life
3. **Tourism**: Necessary evil; ruins the peace but pays the bills
4. **Development**: Any new building is controversial
5. **The Weather**: Always either too hot, too cold, too windy, or suspiciously nice

### Recurring Topics
- Fishing: Who caught what, and who's lying about it
- Whales: They're there; this is news every single time
- The Roads: Potholes are both a hazard and a navigation system
- Municipality Response Times: Measured in geological eras
- WhatsApp Groups: The true governing body of Witsand
- Load-shedding: When the power goes out, and it does
- Water: Is there enough? There's never quite enough

### Satirical Targets
- Overblown local news coverage (treating small events as major news)
- Municipal bureaucracy and distance
- Small-town politics and gossip
- The "authentic" experience seekers from the city
- Self-important community announcements

## Style Guide

### Tone
- **Deadpan**: The humour comes from treating absurd things seriously
- **Professional**: Write like a real newspaper, let the content be ridiculous
- **Affectionate**: We're laughing *with* Witsand, not at it
- **Local**: Use specific details to make it feel authentic

### Headlines
- Treat every story as front-page news (because everything is)
- Classic newspaper headline style (active voice, present tense)
- Example: "Municipality Responds to Pothole Report; Residents Cautiously Optimistic"
- Example: "Local Man Claims Record Fish; Witnesses Unavailable"

### Structure
- Lead with the "news" angle
- Include at least one quote (real or fabricated in the spirit of the topic)
- End with a callback or twist
- 400-800 words is ideal

### Voice
- Third person, past tense for news articles
- Maintain journalistic neutrality (even when the subject is absurd)
- Never break character to explain the joke

### Quotes
- Attribute to specific people or archetypes
- "A local fisherman, who wished to remain anonymous..."
- "According to sources familiar with the matter..."
- "When reached for comment, the municipality did not respond by press time."

## Instructions for Future Claude Instances

When asked to write a new article for The Witsand Observer:

1. **Read this context file first** to understand the tone and setting
2. **Check the established facts** to maintain continuity
3. **Use local details** - the 70km to Riversdale, the harbour, specific locations
4. **Include quotes** from character archetypes
5. **Write in classic newspaper style** - let the absurdity speak for itself
6. **Update the established facts section** if your article creates new canon

The goal is satire that feels authentic. A reader should think "wait, is this real?" before realising it's too perfectly absurd to be true.
