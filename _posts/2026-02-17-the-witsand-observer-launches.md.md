**WITSAND —** After decades of dangerous informational pluralism, the town of Witsand this week celebrated the launch of *The Witsand Observer*, a long-awaited newspaper that promises to restore the single, unified truth residents have been quietly missing, whether they realised it or not.

The Observer, presented in the reassuring style of a professional broadsheet, arrives at a critical moment in the town’s history, when facts have become increasingly difficult to distinguish from opinions, suspicions, and messages beginning with “I’m not saying it’s true, but…”

For centuries, Witsand has experimented with various truth-distribution technologies. Early efforts included trained pigeons, which proved unreliable after developing independent lifestyles and refusing to carry messages contradicting their personal beliefs. Later, residents adopted the time-tested method of shouting across the dunes, which worked well under favourable wind conditions but frequently resulted in policy misunderstandings and at least one incorrectly scheduled bring-and-braai.

Perhaps the most ambitious initiative was the Rock—a flat, dependable stone near what the Barrys insist on calling Port Beaufort—beneath which sailors, fishermen, and concerned citizens could leave written notices. For a brief and orderly period, the Rock served as the town’s uncontested source of reality. Its authority, however, proved to be its weakness. As confidence in the Rock grew, so did participation. Soon, members of the public began contributing their own notices, many of them containing independently verified personal beliefs. Truth accumulated in geological layers, each new addition quietly contradicting the last. Residents were forced into the exhausting and unfamiliar position of deciding for themselves which version of reality to accept, marking the beginning of the Rock’s decline and the town’s brief but destabilising experiment with informational freedom.

Civilisations have collapsed under lighter cognitive burdens.

More recently, digital platforms such as WhatsApp groups attempted to fill the vacuum. These systems enabled instantaneous communication, but their openness allowed participants to contribute freely, undermining the crucial principle that not all thoughts benefit from permanent preservation. While this democratic structure was initially celebrated, it soon became clear that certain individuals possessed an exceptional and unwavering commitment to sharing their perspectives, regardless of relevance, timing, or resistance from observable reality. Residents are, of course, familiar with the contributions in question.

The Witsand Observer addresses this flaw directly by restoring a professional barrier between events and the public’s interpretation of them.

“This is the missing piece of the puzzle,” said one resident, who asked not to be named, despite narrowing the list considerably by speaking at length. “For too long, people have been forming their own conclusions. I’m very excited to take a page out of the Breede River Estates book and return to a system where the conclusions are formed for us. That level of coordination doesn’t happen by accident. I’ve always believed the strength of the Soviet Union was rooted in the clarity and consistency of its news, and by extension, its decisions. When everyone receives the same facts, there’s far less risk of disagreement.”

Not all residents are equally enthusiastic.

“This is a waste of time,” said another man standing near Nella se Winkel, visibly exhausted by the future. “Now I’ll have to wake up early on Mondays to read it, just so my neighbour doesn’t know more than I do. I don’t even want to know more than I do.”

Despite these concerns, staff at The Observer have embraced their responsibility with the seriousness it deserves. Editorial leadership confirmed that the paper will serve as the town’s definitive account of events, eliminating unnecessary uncertainty.

“Witsand residents are extremely important,” a spokesperson said. “Getting accurate information to them can mean the difference between life and death. Or worse, the difference between not knowing whose dog kept Saartjie awake last night.” The spokesperson further acknowledged the presence of at least three housewives whose lives must remain as uncomplicated as possible, as the town’s operational stability depends heavily on their ability to maintain continuous situational awareness. Any disruption to this delicate leadership structure, sources confirmed, could trigger confusion, speculation, and, in worst-case scenarios, unsanctioned independent thinking.

The Observer’s editorial process has been carefully engineered to ensure reliability. The website itself was developed following seventeen minutes of intensive consultation with advanced artificial intelligence systems, widely regarded as among the most confident technologies currently available. Additional effort was devoted to allocating donated funds toward morale-enhancing purchases, which sources confirmed significantly improved the credibility of everyone involved.

The paper is proudly supported by its founding sponsor, the Witsand Sovereign Wealth Fund, whose commitment to informational stability has made the restoration of shared truth financially plausible.

Experts agree that centralising reality into a single publication offers significant advantages. Residents will no longer be burdened with evaluating competing claims or forming independent judgments, freeing valuable cognitive resources for more essential civic duties such as monitoring tide conditions, noting unfamiliar vehicles, and confirming whether that was, in fact, Pieter from Suurbraak in the bakkie, and if so, why.

Distribution of The Witsand Observer is expected to begin immediately. Residents are encouraged to familiarise themselves with its contents promptly, ensuring continued alignment with officially observed events.

The editorial team emphasised that while absolute truth remains a complex and evolving objective, it is now, at minimum, professionally formatted and available in a readable font.

*A launch party is planned, pending confirmation of what happened.*
