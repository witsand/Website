The **Witsand Observer** is a journalistic phenomenon of global significance—or at least of enormous consequence to a village of approximately 600 souls. Until our arrival, Witsand relied on WhatsApp groups, shouting across the dunes, and gossip that spreads like beach sand in the South Easterly wind.  

Founded on the back of a napkin in a local bar in 2026, the idea for the Observer quickly became necessary. At least one million sats were donated without question to get it started—or it may have been just the bar tab that was paid, we'll never know. Tracking these things is difficult in Witsand, where everything is simultaneously historic and ephemeral.

## Our Mission

To deliver the news that matters to Witsand. All of it. Every story is front-page news, because in a village this size, even a misplaced pair of Crocs on the beach can spark a full-scale debate. Getting accurate information to the town’s 600 very important residents is our highest priority.

We understand that the fate of the world may hinge on these reports. No pressure.

## Editorial Standards and Independence

The Witsand Observer maintains complete editorial independence. The WSWF cannot censor us—coordinating hundreds of sovereign individuals is harder than leading a 15-man detachment to Moody se put. We are, however, always open to suggestions if there happens to be a beer on the table.  

Getting this newspaper up and running required 14 minutes of WhatsApp negotiations, 47 messages debating which beer is best, and 17 minutes of prompting AI. One member accidentally sending funds to the wrong address to spin up a server. Democracy, Witsand-style, is slow—but sincere.

Our reporters undergo rigorous training, which consists primarily of living in Witsand and knowing at least three people by name.  

We verify all facts through an extensive and highly trusted network of sources, including the petrol attendant at Nella se Winkel, Johan se Neef, and Piet.

The whale whisperers may once have ruled the news, but this is a new era. Whales, secret societies, and members of the local business elite now provide occasional informal consultancy—always discreetly, sometimes over coffee, and often in the shadow of the cliffs near Moody se put.

## A Note on Satire

While our commitment to truth is unwavering, astute readers may notice that the Observer occasionally employs satire. This is a time-honoured tradition, in part to differentiate ourselves from the Suid-Kaap Forum and other online sources, whose credibility is widely, and quietly, questioned.  

Our goal is simple: to be more truthful than those sources—which, to be fair, is not a particularly high bar.  

Any resemblance to competent journalism is purely coincidental. Any resemblance to actual events is entirely intentional.

## Competitors

Our competitors—both local and global—include South African government-controlled news outlets and the US government’s sprawling propaganda network, which occasionally makes cameo appearances but has yet to produce anything of substance. This makes our editorial independence—and quiet supremacy—remarkably easy to maintain, though we remain open to suggestions if there happens to be a beer on the table.  

The restaurants of Witsand double as our offices and meeting rooms, and strategic planning sometimes occurs while observing whales in their natural habitat—both oceanic and financial.

## Secret Societies

Some of our most active contributors and readers belong to a loosely defined, extremely unofficial secret society. Members are renowned for blending in, sometimes to the point of looking homeless. They ensure the Observer continues to operate at the precarious intersection of chaos and precision.

## Contact

For story tips, corrections, or letters to the editor, please visit our [Submit a Story](/submit) page. An office is unnecessary in a village where every restaurant, bar, beach house, and lookout can double as a newsroom.
